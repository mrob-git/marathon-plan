# Race Dossier — London Marathon 2027

**Date:** Sunday 27 April 2027 (mass start 09:35)
**Distance:** 42.195km / 26.2 miles
**Course:** Point to point, Blackheath to The Mall
**Goal:** A: 3:10 (4:30/km) | B: 3:15 (4:37/km) | C: 3:20 (4:44/km)

---

## 1. The course

### Profile
One of the flattest World Marathon Majors courses. 127m total ascent, 161m descent, 34m net downhill. 85% of the course is essentially flat. ([findmymarathon.com](https://findmymarathon.com/race-detail.php?zname=London+Marathon), [RunReady course guide](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start))

### Section-by-section

**Miles 0-4 (Blackheath to Woolwich) — The descent**
Three colour-coded starts (Blue, Red, Green) merge before mile 3. The first significant feature is the Charlton descent: a 0.72-mile drop of 111 feet at -2.94% grade starting around mile 2.3. This is the steepest sustained descent of any World Major. Runners who hammer the downhill fry their quads and pay for it after mile 20. Lean slightly forward, maintain cadence, resist the free speed. ([RunReady](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start), [Runners Connect](https://runnersconnect.net/race-guides/london-marathon-pace-calculator/))

**Miles 4-6.5 (Greenwich to Cutty Sark) — Settle in**
Flat, wide roads. First major crowd point at the Cutty Sark (~mile 6.5). Tight turns through Greenwich town centre — stay alert, don't clip other runners. This is where marathon rhythm should be locked in. ([Sports Tours International](https://sportstoursinternational.co.uk/blog/london-marathon-route/))

**Miles 6.5-12 (Deptford to Tower Bridge) — The quiet stretch**
Relatively flat through residential South London. Crowd support thins compared to the opening. Minor undulation around mile 10. This is the unglamorous middle section where patience pays. ([RunReady](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start))

**Mile 12-13 (Tower Bridge) — The adrenaline trap**
Iconic crossing. One of the loudest spectator hubs on the course. Slight uphill onto the bridge, downhill off. The noise and emotion tempt runners to surge — this is where sub-3 attempts go wrong. Hold effort, not pace. ([RunReady](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start), [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/london-marathon-mile-by-mile-race-plan/))

**Miles 14-20 (Isle of Dogs / Canary Wharf) — The course within the course**
A winding loop through Docklands. Key challenges:
- **The Charter Street Punch (mile 19.1):** Steepest climb on course — 0.1 miles at 4.22% grade. Shorten stride, pump arms, don't spike HR. ([RunReady](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start))
- **Wind tunnels:** Tall buildings funnel wind into inconsistent gusts. Can be a headwind or swirling crosswind depending on conditions. ([Geeks on Feet race report](https://geeksonfeet.com/blog/london-marathon-23-racereport/))
- **GPS dropout:** Watch pace jumps from 3:40/km to 4:40/km with no change in effort. Tall buildings and tunnels wreck satellite signal. Do NOT chase the watch here — run by feel and check average pace only. ([Carl Topham race report](https://carl-topham.com/articles/london-marathon-24-race-report))
- **Crowd support dips:** Fewer spectators in parts. Psychologically isolating, especially at miles 17-19. ([RunReady](https://runready.substack.com/p/the-2026-london-marathon-blue-start))

**Miles 20-24 (Embankment) — Where races are decided**
Flat, straight, relentless. The Victoria Embankment is continuous level pavement with no topographical relief. This is where glycogen depletion and cumulative quad fatigue from the early descent converge. 19 in 20 London Marathon runners slow in the second half — this is where the fade accelerates. Average slowdown reaches 19% by 35km. ([SWLondoner data analysis](https://www.swlondoner.co.uk/sport/19062026-hitting-the-wall-what-320000-runners-reveal-about-the-london-marathon))
- **Blackfriars Underpass (mile 24):** Brief darkness with a 2.5% incline coming out. Feels harder than it is with 24 miles in the legs. ([gallusrunning.com](https://www.gallusrunning.com/london))

**Miles 24-26.2 (Parliament to The Mall) — The finish**
Big Ben, Parliament, London Eye. Crowd volume surges. Right turn onto The Mall — slightly downhill to the finish at Buckingham Palace. If you've paced well, this is where you push. If you haven't, you'll be clinging on.

### Surface
All tarmac road. No technical terrain, no trail, no cobbles.

### What finishers say
- Cool and overcast is the sweet spot. 2024 (8°C, cloudy) had a near-perfect PR score of 99.38
- 2018 (22°C, sunny) produced the largest percentage slowdown on record. Heat is the biggest race-day risk that's out of your control
- Wind at Canary Wharf is worse than the headline number suggests because the buildings funnel it
- Prepare for anything from 5°C frost to 20°C sunshine. Layering and a disposable top for the start are standard advice

## 3. What finishers warn about

### Where races fall apart
1. **The opening descent (miles 2-3).** Runners go out 10.4% faster than their sustainable pace on the Greenwich downhill. Free speed that costs quad durability later. ([SWLondoner analysis](https://www.swlondoner.co.uk/sport/19062026-hitting-the-wall-what-320000-runners-reveal-about-the-london-marathon))
2. **Tower Bridge adrenaline (mile 12).** The noise tempts a pace surge at halfway. Every second banked here is borrowed from miles 22-26.
3. **Canary Wharf disorientation (miles 14-20).** Winding route, GPS dropout, crowd gaps, wind tunnels. Runners who chase their watch here blow up. Geeks on Feet: "GPS went haywire, threw me off rhythm and focus." ([Geeks on Feet](https://geeksonfeet.com/blog/london-marathon-23-racereport/))
4. **The Embankment (miles 20-24).** Flat and featureless. No crowds to lift you, no turns to break it up. Where glycogen depletion hits. 19/20 runners fade here; average slowdown is 19% off opening pace by 35km. ([SWLondoner](https://www.swlondoner.co.uk/sport/19062026-hitting-the-wall-what-320000-runners-reveal-about-the-london-marathon))
5. **Fuelling failure.** Multiple race reports cite missed gels as the trigger for the wall. The flat course means no forced walking on hills — runners forget to fuel because effort feels steady.

### Common mistakes at the sub-3:15 level
- Going out at 4:25/km because it "feels easy" then dying at 35km
- Chasing GPS pace through Canary Wharf and surging/braking repeatedly
- Not practising race nutrition in training — first time taking gels at race pace
- Underestimating the psychological toll of the Embankment stretch with nothing to look at

## 4. Gear and logistics

### Race day gear (for 8-14°C, partly cloudy)
- **Shoes:** Whatever you've trained your long runs in. Carbon racers are fine if you've raced in them before (you wore them for Vitality — peroneal issue noted, so check stability)
- **Vest/singlet + shorts.** Arm sleeves for the start, ditch if it warms up
- **Disposable long-sleeve top** for the 60-90 min wait at the start. Bin it before you cross the line
- **Cap** if sunny, skip if overcast
- **Vaseline** on nipples, inner thighs, feet
- **Race belt** for gels — practise in training
- **Watch:** Set to show average pace, not live pace (GPS will lie at Canary Wharf)

### Nutrition
- Aid stations every 1-2 miles: Buxton water + Lucozade Sport
- Carry your own gels. 60g carbs/hour minimum. Every 20-25 minutes from mile 3
- Practise the exact brand, flavour and timing on every long run from 12 weeks out

### Logistics
- **Expo (packet pickup):** ExCeL London (Custom House station, DLR/Elizabeth line). Opens Wednesday before the race. Go Wednesday or Thursday — Friday and Saturday are packed. You must collect in person.
- **Race day travel:** Free TFL travel with race bib (Underground, Overground, DLR, buses). No driving — extensive road closures. Plan to arrive at the start 90 min before gun time.
- **Bag drop:** Official kitbag only (collected at expo). Attach your number sticker. Drop-off and pickup are straightforward.
- **Start:** Blackheath. Three starts merge before mile 3. Mass start at 09:35. You'll be standing around for 60-90 min in the assembly area — dress for waiting, not running.

### Travel from Nottingham
- Train: Nottingham to St Pancras (East Midlands Railway, ~1h 40m). Then Underground/DLR to start
- Consider travelling down Saturday, staying near Greenwich or central London. Do not travel on race morning — too much can go wrong
- Expo on Friday or Saturday if travelling down the day before

## 5. What this race demands of ME

### Where I'm ready
- **The flat course suits me.** My easy running is aerobically efficient (6:16/km at 141 HR post-10K race). A flat course rewards a steady engine, which is what I'm building.
- **Pacing discipline.** I ran the Vitality 10K at 4:24/km, bang on target. I run easy days genuinely easy (135-141 HR). I don't surge. The London course punishes surgers and rewards patient runners — that's my profile.
- **Heat resilience isn't tested yet, but cool conditions are more likely.** Average race day is 11°C at the start. I train in Nottingham through winter. Cool and overcast would suit me.
- **Race execution under pressure.** Vitality showed I can hold a plan under race conditions. 43:56 against a 44:00 target, well-paced, full effort.

### Where I'm exposed
- **Volume is the biggest gap.** CTL is 27. Typical marathon finishers targeting 3:10-3:20 have CTL in the 50-70 range. Weekly volume has been 25-35km with frequent dips to single digits. I need to be consistently running 50-60km/week for months before the marathon-specific block, and I'm not there yet.
- **No marathon-distance long runs.** Longest non-race run in recent months is ~12km (58 min). The race is 42.2km. Even with the Norwegian Singles long run cap at 60-75 min during the development phase, I'll need to build well beyond that in the marathon block. I have zero experience of running beyond 90 minutes at any pace.
- **Durability beyond 25km is proven but from a lower fitness base.** Berlin Marathon 2024 in 3:37 (5:09/km) shows I can handle the full distance. But London targets are 30-40 sec/km faster, which means significantly higher glycogen burn rate and more muscular stress. The last 10km at 4:30-4:44/km is a different physiological demand to the last 10km at 5:09/km.
- **Fuelling at race pace is proven at 120g/hr.** Took 3 SiS Beta Fuel gels per hour at Berlin with no GI issues. This is a genuine strength — most runners can't do this. Fueling is not a risk area.
- **Injury pattern vs training load required.** Every intensity introduction has produced a niggle (knee Base W3, hamstring after NSM reps, calf before 10K, peroneal from race shoes). The training load required for a 3:10-3:20 marathon means months of sustained higher volume AND quality. Managing this without breaking down is the central challenge.
- **No recent half marathon race result.** A half marathon would validate the faster marathon pace targets. 10K at 43:56 and Berlin 3:37 (2024) give a baseline, but a half in the 1:30-1:35 range would confirm 3:10-3:20 potential at the new fitness level.

### Honest assessment
Berlin 3:37 in 2024 is the baseline. The 10K improvement (43:56 at Vitality vs whatever the Berlin training cycle produced) and the aerobic efficiency gains visible in the Strava data suggest genuine fitness progression since then. A 17-27 minute improvement (3:37 → 3:10-3:20) is large but not unreasonable over 2.5 years with structured training.

The A goal (3:10, 4:30/km) is ambitious. It requires roughly doubling CTL, building long run capacity back to 32-35km at a faster pace, and maintaining that for months without injury. Possible but demands a near-perfect block.

The B goal (3:15, 4:37/km) is realistic if the next 6 months go well — consistent volume building, successful introduction of sub-threshold work, a strong marathon-specific block, and arriving healthy. A 22-minute improvement on Berlin with better training is achievable.

The C goal (3:20, 4:44/km) is achievable with a solid but imperfect block. A 17-minute improvement on Berlin with room for a missed week or two.

## 7. What this means I need to train

Based on the gaps above, ranked by priority:

1. **Consistent volume before anything else.** Get weekly km into a narrow, repeatable band (35-40km) for 4+ weeks before thinking about progression. The coaching philosophy is clear: consistency beats peak load. This is the single biggest determinant of whether I reach the start line healthy.

2. **Build long run capacity gradually.** From current ~60 min to the 75 min NSM ceiling over the next 8-10 weeks. Then in the marathon block, extend progressively toward 2h30-2h45 (28-35km). No jumps, drop-back every 4th week.

3. **Earn the sub-threshold sessions.** First quality session gated on 35km/week + CTL 30 + zero niggles. Build from 1 to 2 to 3 sessions per week over months, not weeks. This is where the aerobic engine gets built.

4. **Race a half marathon.** Target January or February 2027. This validates marathon pace targets, tests durability beyond 10K, and practises fuelling and pacing at race effort for 90+ minutes. A half in the 1:32-1:36 range would confirm 3:10-3:20 marathon potential.

5. **Practise race fuelling.** From the moment long runs exceed 60 min, start taking gels. 60g carbs/hour, every 20-25 min. Same brand, same timing, every long run. Non-negotiable.

6. **Manage the peroneal.** If carbon racers caused the issue, test stability options well before race day. Do not debut race shoes on 27 April.

7. **Course-specific prep.** Set watch to average pace before the race. Memorise km splits for A/B/C goal paces. Study the Canary Wharf loop so GPS dropout doesn't cause panic. Run one sustained flat effort (tempo or long run) on a flat route (Trent Embankment, Victoria Embankment canal path) to calibrate flat-ground pacing — Mapperley hills distort pace/HR data.
