## The math

### Carbohydrates

**Formula:** body weight (kg) × 1g/hr = baseline, capped by gut tolerance

- Baseline: 71 kg × 1 = 71g/hr
- Gut tolerance ceiling: 120g/hr (race-tested at Berlin Marathon 2024, full distance, no GI issues)
- **Target: 120g/hr**

Why 120g/hr? Because it's proven. Mike took 3 Beta Fuel gels per hour for the full Berlin Marathon at race effort with zero stomach issues. This is not a guess or a training-pace number — it's a race-validated intake. SiS Beta Fuel's 1:0.8 maltodextrin:fructose ratio maximises dual-transport oxidation at this rate. Recent research on elite marathoners confirms higher exogenous carbohydrate oxidation and improved running economy at 120g/hr vs 90g/hr ([J Applied Physiology, 2025](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/prev/20251203-aop/abs/10.1152/japplphysiol.00665.2025)).

**Total carbs needed:**
- At 3:15 (middle goal): 3.25 hrs × 120g/hr = **390g total**
- SiS Beta Fuel gels: 40g each. 3 per hour = 120g/hr from gels
- Lucozade Sport at aid stations provides additional top-up
- **Plan: 10 gels + Lucozade at 2-3 stations = ~400-440g total**

### Fluid

**Sweat rate estimate:**

⚠️ ESTIMATE — not measured. Using body weight scaling from 800 mL/hr baseline at 70kg in 10-15°C:

- Sweat rate = (71 / 70) × 800 = **~815 mL/hr**
- At 10°C (cool): no temperature adjustment → **~815 mL/hr**
- At 15°C (mild): no adjustment → **~815 mL/hr**
- At 20°C (warm): +15% → **~940 mL/hr**

**How to measure the real number:** Weigh yourself nude before and after a 60-min run at marathon effort (4:35/km) in 10-15°C. Don't drink during the test. Don't urinate. (Pre-weight − post-weight) = sweat loss in kg = litres. Repeat in warmer conditions (18-20°C) to get a heat-adjusted figure.

**Replacement target:** 70-80% of sweat rate (do not try to replace 100%)

- Cool day (10°C): 815 × 0.75 = **~610 mL/hr** = ~2 cups per aid station, hitting every other station
- Mild day (15°C): 815 × 0.75 = **~610 mL/hr** (same)
- Warm day (20°C): 940 × 0.75 = **~705 mL/hr** = more frequent station stops

**Dehydration ceiling:** 71 kg × 2% = 1.42 kg maximum acceptable weight loss

London Marathon aid stations: water every mile from mile 3, Lucozade Sport at selected stations. No need to carry a bottle — grab a cup at every other water station.

### Sodium

**Formula:** sweat rate (L/hr) × sweat sodium (mg/L) = sodium need (mg/hr)

⚠️ ESTIMATE — sweat sodium not measured. Using 900 mg/L (population average, appropriate for non-heavy sweater with no cramping history):

- 0.815 L/hr × 900 mg/L = **~735 mg/hr sodium**

**SiS Beta Fuel gel sodium content:** ~55mg per gel. At 2 gels/hr = 110 mg/hr from gels. Shortfall: ~625 mg/hr.

**How to close the gap:**
- Lucozade Sport: ~250mg sodium per 500ml. 1-2 servings helps
- Salt tablet option: SiS GO Electrolyte tab or Precision Hydration PH 500 capsule (500mg sodium). One per hour would bring total to ~610-860 mg/hr — within range
- Or: take an electrolyte drink (500mg+ sodium) in the last hour before the start as a pre-load

**Recommendation:** 1 salt capsule per hour (carried in shorts pocket or taped to gel packet), plus sodium from Lucozade at aid stations. Total: ~700-900 mg/hr. Within the target range.

**How to measure the real number:** Precision Hydration sweat test (~£35) gives your individual sweat sodium concentration. Worth doing in training — book at [precisionhydration.com](https://www.precisionhydration.com).

### Warm day adjustment (>18°C)
If race morning is warm:
- Add 1 water station stop per 5km segment (drink at every station, not every other)
- Add a 4th salt capsule at km 35
- Consider slowing target pace by 5-10 sec/km (see pacing heat adjustment below)
- The sauna/hot bath acclimation helps but doesn't eliminate heat impact

## Pacing plan

### Strategy: even effort, not even pace

The London course is 85% flat with a significant early descent and minor rises at miles 19 and 24. The optimal strategy is to run by effort through the descent and rises, accepting pace variation, then lock into rhythm on the flat. Permission to push from km 37 if feeling strong.

GPS will be unreliable through Canary Wharf (km 23-32). Switch watch display to average pace before the race.

### Segment plan (B goal — 3:15, 4:37/km)

| Segment | Km | Target pace | Cumulative time | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Start + descent** | 0-6.5 | 4:35-4:40/km | ~30:00 | Controlled | Resist the downhill. 5-8 sec/km slower than you want. Let others go. |
| **Settle in** | 6.5-12 | 4:37/km | ~55:30 | Rhythm | Lock in. This is your pace for the next 2 hours. |

### Heat adjustment
If race-day temperature exceeds 18°C:
- Add 5 sec/km to all segments for the first 25km
- Reassess at halfway — if HR is elevated beyond normal marathon effort, add another 5 sec/km
- Better to run 3:25 in the heat than DNF chasing 3:15

## Pre-race nutrition

### Day before
- 8-10g carbs per kg body weight = **570-710g carbs** across the day
- White rice, pasta, bread, bagels, cereal, fruit juice. Low fibre, low fat
- Sodium-rich dinner (add salt to food) to aid fluid retention
- Sip water throughout the day. Urine should be pale yellow by evening

### Race morning (3 hours before start — ~06:30)
- 2-3g carbs per kg = **140-210g carbs**
- Familiar foods only: porridge, toast with jam, banana, sports drink
- 500 mL water with breakfast
- Stop eating 2 hours before gun time
- 45 min before start: 1 Beta Fuel gel + 250 mL electrolyte drink (sodium pre-load)
